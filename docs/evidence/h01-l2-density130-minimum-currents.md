# Local recovery currents identify a potassium path to test

At each of the four recorded recovery minima, Kv3_1 is the largest of
the recorded outward ionic current densities at soma(0.5): 0.273985,
0.248758, 0.239243, and 0.242506 mA/cm2. These are local samples at
each event's own minimum. They are not whole-cell currents or a
same-time comparison with the human cell.

The source uses ik = gbar*m*(v-ek), with a gate that relaxes toward its
voltage-dependent equilibrium. Reconstruction from current and voltage
shows the gate remains above its equilibrium value at these minima.
This is consistent with residual potassium conductance after a spike.
It does not show that changing this path will correct recovery while
preserving peaks, falling phases, and later spike times.

A voltage minimum does not require each ionic current to vanish. Local
capacitive current is near zero here while ionic, injected, and axial
currents can balance. Axial current was not directly recorded; these
samples do not independently close the compartment current balance.

The observations motivate a controlled Kv3 closing-law intervention,
subject to source checks and numerical validation. They do not prove
that Kv3 is the unique cause of the human mismatch. Full current samples
are in [the current record](h01-l2-density130-minimum-currents.json);
source-law reconstructions are in [the source review](h01-l2-density130-kv3-source-review.json).
