# Delayed-cue temporal-credit benchmark

Status: implementation specification
Master manifest seed: `20260810`
Bootstrap seed: `20260811`

## Claim boundary

Example 17 tests whether single-step pp-prop learns recurrent edges that are
temporally useful on a delayed-cue-recall task beyond a matched frozen random
reservoir. A passing release supports only that claim. It does not establish
superiority to BPTT, general continual learning, or brain-scale coupled
recurrence. Full-window reverse mode is always reported as a BPTT oracle and is
never labelled pp-prop.

Examples 15 and 16 remain the static-learning and scaling controls. Example 16
schema 2 adds explicit topology metadata while retaining every numerical
default from schema 1. Historical schema-1 results remain valid.

## Task

Each trial has 17 binary inputs: two disjoint eight-channel cue ensembles and
one common go channel. Class cues have equal rate and duration. Spike
probability is derived from physical rate:

`p = 1 - exp(-rate_hz * dt_seconds)`.

The three horizons are:

| Horizon | Cue | Silence | Response | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| short | 4 | 2 | 4 | 10 |
| medium | 4 | 22 | 4 | 30 |
| long | 4 | 92 | 4 | 100 |

Only the response window is supervised. It contains the label-independent go
cue and no class cue. Core horizon experiments contain no delay noise, so all
horizons have the same information and supervision budgets.

The feed-forward synapse, LIF membrane, and leaky readout use a fixed 0.5 ms
time constant; the recurrent synapse uses 3 ms. At the 1 ms benchmark tick, an
inactive recurrent trace decays by `exp(-92/3)` across the long delay and
is far below the spike threshold. This prevents a fixed filter, membrane,
readout, or passive recurrent trace from acting as an analog delay line; useful
long-delay activity must be regenerated by recurrent spiking. These time
constants are fixed construction choices, not development search knobs.

The balanced split sizes are 1,024 train, 256 validation, and 512 test trials.
Test trial specifications are stored as sealed commitments until the
configuration, gain, optimizer, trace, and acceptance logic are frozen. The
runner refuses test evaluation for a dirty source tree or an unsealed result.
Every sealed run also requires the frozen development-selection artifact. The
runner validates its schema, exact source commit, container image digest, and
construction dimensions, then derives every selected optimization setting from
that artifact; command-line defaults or overrides cannot silently replace a
frozen selection. The sealed-matrix driver runs one isolated subprocess per
arm and bundle, validates an existing raw result before reuse, and fails closed
unless all seven arms and all twelve bundles are present.

## Paired arms

Every paired bundle starts from identical topology, initial weights, trial
order, and encoded spikes.

1. `readout_only`: train readout; freeze feed-forward and recurrence.
2. `feedforward_readout_recurrence_zero`: train feed-forward and readout; force
   recurrent contribution to zero.
3. `recurrent_readout`: train recurrence and readout; freeze feed-forward.
4. `all_pp_prop`: train feed-forward, recurrence, and readout with single-step
   pp-prop.
5. `no_recurrent_module`: remove recurrence; train feed-forward and readout.
6. `frozen_random_recurrence`: keep random recurrence active; train
   feed-forward and readout.
7. `all_bptt`: train all parameter groups with full-window reverse mode.

Arms 2 and 5 must agree within the configured numerical tolerance. BPTT uses
the same optimizer policy and sample budget.

## Randomness and topology

The committed manifest contains 12 bundles: three split seeds by two topology
seeds by two weight seeds. Each bundle independently derives
`training_order_seed`, `training_encoding_seed`, and eight shared
`evaluation_encoding_seeds`. Seed domains are explicit and collision-checked.

Scientific topology is fixed out-degree, independently sampled without
replacement per row, with no self-loops and O(neurons * degree) construction.
Recurrent values use a distinct weight seed and standard deviation
`gain / sqrt(degree)`. The development-only gain candidates are 0.5, 0.8, 1.0,
and 1.2. Selection orders candidates by highest ensemble validation accuracy,
then lowest NLL, then lowest gain.

Gain selection uses the all-parameter pp-prop arm, direct long-horizon
training for 800 updates, the provisional learning rates 0.003, 0.001, and
0.0003, long-horizon trace half-lives 60/60, recurrent weight decay zero, and
clip norm 1.0. It runs on the same three development bundles named below and
ranks mean validation accuracy descending, mean validation NLL ascending, and
gain ascending. The selected gain is then held fixed during learning-rate
successive halving. This is a declared coordinate-search order, not a joint
global optimum claim.

Topology telemetry includes row, in-degree, and out-degree statistics;
duplicate and self-loop counts; weak and strong component counts; largest
component fraction; and a named sparse power-gain estimate. It must never be
described as an exact spectral radius.

Example 16 schema 2 declares
`topology_family = "legacy_translated_offsets"` and reports its realized
self-loop count. This is descriptive only and must not alter construction or
defaults.

## Optimization and dynamics

After task validity, readout, feed-forward, and recurrent parameters use
disjoint Adam state. Biases and thresholds are frozen in the primary benchmark.
The learning-rate schedule has a 10% linear warmup from 10% peak, is constant
through 70%, and cosine-decays to 15% peak. Initial clip norm is 1.0 per group.

The successive-halving grid is:

- readout: 0.001, 0.003, 0.01;
- feed-forward: 0.0003, 0.001, 0.003;
- recurrent: 0.0001, 0.0003, 0.001;
- stages: all 27 for 100 updates, best nine for 300, best three for 800 on
  three development bundles.

Non-finite and stability-invalid runs are rejected. Survivors rank by
validation NLL, ensemble accuracy, then recurrent update ratio. Recurrent-only
weight decay is selected afterwards from 0, 1e-5, and 1e-4. If a group clips on
more than 25% of updates, compare 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, and disabled.

The final 800-update stage promotes its first-ranked candidate as the unique
learning-rate winner. Weight-decay candidates then run direct long-horizon
training for 800 updates on all three development bundles with the winning
gain and learning rates. They use the same NLL-ascending,
accuracy-descending, recurrent-ratio-ascending rule. Clip telemetry is then
measured from the winning weight-decay runs. For each parameter group whose
clip-event fraction exceeds 0.25, compare that group's candidates at 800
updates while holding the other groups at their selected values; use the same
ranking rule. Clip norms are stored per parameter group, and `disabled` is
represented by a null norm rather than an arbitrary large number.

The development bundles are fixed before search as
`split0-topology0-weight0`, `split1-topology0-weight0`, and
`split2-topology0-weight0`, giving one validation split per split seed while
leaving every committed test trial sealed. Each stage starts deterministically
from the bundle's initial state and trains for that stage's full update budget;
later stages do not inherit weights from shorter stages. A candidate must be
finite and stability-valid on all three bundles. Ranking uses mean validation
NLL ascending, mean ensemble accuracy descending, and the mean of each
bundle's recurrent update-ratio p99 ascending, followed by the ordered grid as
a deterministic final tie-break. If rejection leaves fewer candidates than a
stage's promotion count, all valid survivors advance; zero survivors fails the
search closed.

Development search execution is subprocess-isolated by candidate and bundle.
Every raw run is retained, existing runs are reused only after their full
configuration is validated, and a schema-versioned stage summary is written
after each completed stage. This makes an interrupted search resumable without
silently mixing configurations. Search results are development-only and must
never materialize sealed test metrics.

Only `trace_half_life_x_steps` and `trace_half_life_f_steps` are exposed. Each
decay is `2**(-1/H)`. Integer rank is not a benchmark knob. Half-life grids are
short 5/10, medium 10/20/30, and long 30/60/100.

Half-lives are selected after optimizer settings. For each horizon, direct
training uses that horizon's maximum curriculum budget (200, 400, or 800) on
the three development bundles. Coordinate selection is deterministic: first
search X while F is held at the provisional value 10, 20, or 60, then search F
while X is held at its selected value. Candidates use the same
NLL-ascending, accuracy-descending, recurrent-ratio-ascending rule and must be
finite and stability-valid on all bundles. This coordinate search is reported
as such and does not claim a factorial or global optimum. The curriculum must
consume the independently selected X/F pair for each phase rather than replace
it with equal hard-coded values.

The curriculum is short, medium, long with 200, 400, and 800 update ceilings.
Weights and Adam moments carry between phases; hidden and eligibility state
reset only at boundaries. A phase promotes after validation ensemble accuracy
is at least 0.80 at two consecutive checkpoints and stability gates pass.

Curriculum adoption is decided on the three fixed development bundles only. A
sample-tick is one trial sample advanced by one physical timestep, so an update
consumes `batch_size * horizon.total_steps` sample-ticks. For each bundle, the
matched direct-long control starts from a fresh copy of the same seeded initial
state and runs for exactly the sample-ticks actually consumed by that bundle's
curriculum. The comparison fails closed unless this budget is an integer number
of long-horizon updates. It records the arithmetic for every phase and control.

Time-to-0.80 is the first long-horizon validation checkpoint that reaches 0.80
while passing the stability gates; preceding short and medium sample-ticks count
toward curriculum time. The time comparison is complete only when both methods
reach the threshold on every development bundle. Final long-delay accuracy uses
a paired 10,000-resample hierarchical interval with seed `20260811`. Curriculum
is adopted only if all paired runs are stable, Example 15 changes by no worse
than -0.01 accuracy, and either complete mean time-to-0.80 improves by at least
20% or the paired long-delay accuracy interval has a lower bound above zero.
Raw comparison runs and the schema-versioned adoption decision are explicitly
development-only and never materialize sealed test trials or metrics.

The Example 15 non-inferiority input comes from a separate fixed-profile static
control document. A baseline is admissible only when its schema records the
unchanged three-seed Example 15 configuration, a clean source commit, an image
digest, passing Example 15 acceptance checks, and an explicit baseline-acceptance
operation. Comparison additionally requires the caller to pin the complete
baseline artifact by SHA-256. The current run may be executed by the helper or
ingested, but must use the identical fixed profile and record source/image
provenance. The reported change is current mean held-out accuracy minus baseline
mean held-out accuracy. Prose tables, threshold-only tests, source similarity,
and unpinned numeric values are not baseline evidence. The curriculum CLI keeps
a raw numeric override for development diagnostics, while the validated static
control artifact is the preferred adoption input.

## Instrumentation and gradient evidence

Every parameter group reports raw and clipped gradient norms, clipping, weight
norm, update norm, update-to-weight ratio, Adam moment norms, and effective
learning rate. Dynamics report firing-rate mean and percentiles, silent and
saturated fractions, membrane-voltage percentiles, and recurrent power gain.

On the 24-neuron degree-4 reference, pp-prop and BPTT recurrent gradients are
compared at initialization, 25%, and 75% of training. Report cosine similarity,
sign agreement, norm ratio, relative deviation, and downstream loss change
after identical all-group updates with a norm equal to 1% of the current total
parameter norm. Recurrent gradients supply the direction metrics; the matched
all-group perturbation supplies the downstream loss-change metric. Gradient
evidence uses finite-window pp-prop, not a vacuous whole-sequence VJP.

## Statistical gates

All confidence intervals use a fixed 10,000-resample hierarchical paired
bootstrap with seed `20260811`, resampling splits and paired bundles. Raw bundle
results are always retained.

| Gate | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Task validity | BPTT long-delay accuracy lower 95% bound at least 0.80; readout-only and feed-forward/no-recurrence upper bounds at most 0.60; response inputs analytically label-independent. |
| pp-prop learning | pp-prop accuracy lower bound above 0.50 and paired accuracy difference versus frozen random recurrence lower bound above zero. |
| Gradient evidence | recurrent cosine advantage over a permuted null has lower bound above zero; matched pp-prop small-update downstream loss change has upper bound below zero. |
| Stability | finite values; mean firing in [0.001, 0.30]; silent fraction at most 0.95; saturated fraction below 0.05; recurrent update-ratio p99 at most 0.05. |
| Streaming | selected no-reset cadence is above chance and has positive paired accuracy difference versus reset-every-window. |

Streaming failure blocks cadence-engine work but not an otherwise valid episodic
benchmark publication.

## Streaming protocol

The separate A-to-B-to-A protocol uses disjoint cue ensembles, no hidden or
eligibility resets, and 400k/400k/200k sample-tick segments. At one million
total sample-ticks it compares cadence K in 1/5/10/30 and batch size in
1/4/16/32. A fresh evaluator runs every 50k sample-ticks. Reports include
adaptation latency, forgetting after B, reacquisition latency, final average
accuracy, throughput, and memory, with reset-every-window and no-plasticity
controls.

## Reproducibility and release

The tracked GPU recipe pins Python 3.14, JAX/JAXlib 0.11.0, CUDA dependencies,
and BrainTrace extras. Results record image digest, NVIDIA driver, CUDA, device,
source commit, dirty state, and package versions.

One raw JSON is emitted per run. Publication creates a manifest,
environment fingerprints, and `SHA256SUMS`, then an immutable compressed
artifact. Git retains the manifest, hashes, methods, and compact report.
Packaging refuses non-finite JSON, dirty sealed results, missing files, hash
mismatches, and failed scientific gates.

Only after BrainTrace CI, examples, typing, build, and sealed gates pass may the
accepted commit be merged and downstream dependency pins changed. The
connectome-agent pin must name that exact published commit, followed by a clean
reinstall, `direct_url.json` verification, tiny gradient smoke, and root checks.

## Explicit follow-on boundaries

Update cadence, matrix-free coupled recurrence, and mixed precision each require
a separate co-located specification, tests, commit, and gate. They are not part
of Example 17's initial claim. Cadence requires exact K=1/K=5/boundary agreement;
matrix-free recurrence requires dense-oracle agreement and a guarded
139,255-neuron run; precision retains FP32 master state and requires at least
10% speed or memory improvement, gradient cosine at least 0.99, and at most one
accuracy point degradation.
